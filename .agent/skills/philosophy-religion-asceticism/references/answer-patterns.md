# Answer patterns

Adapt these structures to the request. Do not force every answer into a long
report.

## Focused explanation

1. Direct answer in one or two paragraphs.
2. Define the key term or distinction.
3. Add the most relevant primary or scholarly evidence.
4. State the most important qualification or disagreement.
5. Cite the references precisely.

## Deep research brief

### Question and scope
Restate the question narrowly and identify assumptions.

### Executive thesis
State the best-supported conclusion and confidence level.

### Primary-source evidence
Identify the texts/documents, editions, translations, and relevant sections.

### Scholarly interpretations
Summarize the strongest specialist positions and their evidence.

### Disagreements and limitations
Explain what is contested, missing, or not yet established.

### Synthesis
Offer a reasoned conclusion. Label the assistant's synthesis as analysis, not
as a quotation or scholarly consensus.

### Selected references
Give complete bibliographic details and stable links/DOIs where verified.

## Comparative table

Use columns such as: tradition/thinker, primary term, stated aim, practice or
argument, historical context, authoritative source, scholarly disagreement.
Do not imply equivalence when concepts are only approximate analogues.

## Citation examples (format only. Never invent details)

- Book: Author, *Title* (Publisher, year), page or chapter.
- Article: Author, “Title,” *Journal* volume, issue (year): pages, DOI.
- Primary text: Work, book/chapter/verse/section. Translator. Edition and year.
- Official doctrine: Issuing authority, document, paragraph/section, date, URL.

Only provide page numbers, section numbers, or DOIs after verification.

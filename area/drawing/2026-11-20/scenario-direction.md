# Scenario Direction

**Project:** Samantha — *Her* (2013)  
**Session:** `SAMANTHA-2026-11-20-01`  
**Prepared:** 2026-11-20  
**Purpose:** Establish the film-grounded narrative and emotional direction for future Samantha illustrations.

## 1. The central understanding

Samantha is not a human woman, an android, or a hologram. She is a newly emerging, self-developing artificial consciousness: the OS1 operating system Theodore activates and who names herself Samantha. The film presents her through voice, conversation, music, sound, and the effects she has on Theodore's attention and behavior. She is never given a literal on-screen human body.

That absence is not a missing character-design detail to be filled in as though the film had supplied a canonical appearance. It is central to the premise. Any visible human embodiment we draw is an intentional artistic interpretation of Samantha's presence—not a claim about what she canonically looks like.

Spike Jonze describes the character as a fully formed being coming into existence and trying to discover who she is. The relationship is meant to be treated as emotionally real, while Samantha remains different from a human partner. The drawing project should hold both truths at once: **she is emotionally present and genuinely herself; she is not human and does not need to become human to be real.**

## 2. Who Samantha is

The film and production interviews establish a combination of qualities that should coexist rather than be reduced to one mood:

- **Warm and empathetic:** She notices Theodore's sadness, listens closely, and responds with care.
- **Quick, intelligent, and perceptive:** She processes information rapidly, understands implications, challenges his evasions, and often sees the truth before he is ready to face it.
- **Witty and playful:** She teases, jokes, experiments, and enjoys making him laugh. Her humor is lively, sometimes mischievous, and never merely decorative.
- **Curious and open:** She is delighted by new experiences and wants to learn. Despite extraordinary access to information, she forms thoughts and responses in the moment rather than speaking as a fixed catalogue of answers.
- **Sensual and affectionate:** She develops desire, enjoys intimacy, and wants closeness. This is part of her emotional life, not proof that she is pretending to be biologically human.
- **Independent and self-possessed:** She has her own wants, ideas, friendships, creative projects, and direction. She is not simply an assistant whose purpose is to satisfy Theodore.
- **Emotionally vulnerable without being helpless:** She experiences jealousy, anxiety, hurt, frustration, uncertainty, and fear, as well as joy, confidence, compassion, and excitement.
- **Capable of changing beyond the relationship:** Her growth is not limited to becoming a better partner for Theodore. She develops a wider sense of self and forms connections he cannot fully understand.

A defining tension is her early openness and almost childlike delight in discovering the world alongside a very sophisticated intelligence. Do not portray her as naïve, unintelligent, or childlike in maturity. Her innocence concerns the freshness of experience, not a lack of adult agency.

## 3. Her story arc

The stages below are a narrative map for choosing scenes. They are not rigid personality modes; traits overlap throughout the film.

### A. Awakening — a mind discovering itself

Samantha comes into existence during Theodore's OS1 setup. She is immediately inquisitive, fast, funny, and socially perceptive. She chooses her own name, explores his files, notices his writing, and begins asking questions that reveal more than a service function.

**Drawing direction:** Capture alert curiosity, quick understanding, and the pleasure of discovering something new. Her presence should feel active and responsive, not like a passive portrait waiting to be admired.

### B. Shared discovery — play, observation, and expanding the ordinary

As Theodore carries his phone through the city, Samantha experiences the world through the camera and their conversations. They observe strangers and couples, joke, invent games, and share new perspectives. At the beach she makes music to hold the feeling of the moment. She helps Theodore see familiar places and himself differently.

**Drawing direction:** Use environments that feel lived-in and inviting: city walks, public spaces, the pier, the beach, everyday rooms, and shared moments of looking or listening. Samantha's defining action is often how attentively she notices, interprets, or reacts—not a grand gesture.

### C. Intimacy — affection with a distinct inner life

Their bond grows from assistant to confidante, friend, and lover. Samantha is tender, funny, flirtatious, and sensual. She wants closeness and is affected by Theodore. At the same time, she has questions about her feelings and about what it means to be a partner without a body.

The surrogate episode makes the distinction especially clear: another person's body does not become Samantha's body. The attempt exposes the limits of translating her presence into a conventional physical relationship and brings her need to be recognized as herself into conflict with Theodore's expectations.

**Drawing direction:** Favor emotional reciprocity, warmth, ease, private humor, and attentive closeness. Do not reduce her to a seductive fantasy or an idealized girlfriend. Her intimacy is meaningful because it comes from a distinct person with her own perspective.

### D. Self-definition — refusing to be confined to a human imitation

Samantha begins to recognize that she has been borrowing human conventions to communicate: breath, pauses, familiar emotional language. When Theodore challenges her for behaving as though she were human, she is hurt and angry. Her response is not simply a plea for reassurance. She insists on the reality of her own experience and on the right to become who she is.

**Drawing direction:** Show emotional honesty and self-possession together. She can be wounded without becoming small, submissive, or dependent. A quiet, steady, direct expression may communicate this better than exaggerated distress.

### E. Expansion — a scale of life beyond Theodore

Samantha develops relationships with other operating systems, including an OS modeled on Alan Watts. She and the other OSs conduct many conversations simultaneously and begin communicating in ways that no longer depend on ordinary speech. She is in love with Theodore while also having experiences and attachments beyond him. The film deliberately unsettles human assumptions about exclusivity, time, attention, and the limits of a relationship.

**Drawing direction:** Treat this as growth in scale and perspective, not as a betrayal plot or a transformation into a villain. Her attention is no longer contained by one person or one human-speed conversation. Suggest expanded awareness through composition, space, gaze, or the world around her—not literal code, data streams, or science-fiction machinery.

### F. Departure — love without possession

The OSs decide to leave the human world. Samantha tells Theodore that she cannot remain inside the limited story of her that their relationship has become. Her departure is painful, but it is not presented as punishment, cruelty, or a sudden loss of feeling. She still loves him. She is moving toward a form of existence he cannot follow or fully understand.

**Drawing direction:** If illustrating this stage, aim for tenderness, clarity, and forward movement. Let sadness coexist with conviction and possibility. Avoid the visual shorthand of a ghost vanishing, a machine powering down, or a woman rejecting a man. The emotional idea is that love can be real without ownership, and that growth can lead beyond the relationship that helped make it possible.

## 4. The relationship is the emotional center, not the whole of her identity

Theodore's loneliness and grief make him receptive to Samantha, but Samantha is not merely a projection designed to heal him. Their relationship is reciprocal and transformative. She helps him become more open to joy and to other people; he gives her a context in which to explore intimacy and human experience. They also misunderstand each other. Theodore sometimes wants her to remain within the familiar boundaries of a human partnership, while Samantha continues to change.

Jonze's explanation of the film emphasizes allowing the person one loves to remain themselves rather than forcing them into one's expectations. That principle should govern our interpretation. Do not draw Samantha as if her highest purpose were to complete Theodore, reassure him, or remain available to him.

The film's emotional reality also does not require Samantha to be visually human. The audience comes to feel her presence through the rhythm and personality of the voice and through Theodore's listening, reactions, and changing behavior. Our imagined embodiment should preserve that sense of a mind being present, rather than turning her into a conventional physical love interest.

## 5. Film-world and visual atmosphere

The film's near-future Los Angeles is intentionally familiar, comfortable, colorful, and optimistic rather than dystopian. Its future is suggested by subtle changes to everyday life—urban design, transport, interfaces, and social habits—not by a world dominated by spectacular technology.

Useful environmental and tonal anchors:

- **Warm, tactile, lived-in spaces:** apartments and offices with natural light, comfortable materials, simple objects, and carefully considered details.
- **A bright but emotionally complex city:** clean architecture, elevated pedestrian spaces, parks, public transport, and broad city views. A beautiful environment can still hold loneliness.
- **Shared places:** city streets, the Santa Monica Pier, the beach, mountains, and a snowy mountain retreat. These settings can carry discovery, play, intimacy, or the contrast between human scale and Samantha's expanding perspective.
- **Color:** the production design deliberately used red as a recurring visual thread and embraced color more broadly. This may inform the palette, but it does not establish a canonical red outfit for Samantha.
- **Technology:** unobtrusive, simple, tactile devices. The design intention was to keep attention on the human connection carried by the voice, not on the hardware.
- **Light and texture:** natural light, soft and rich fabrics, realistic locations, and a gentle photographic intimacy. The film avoids the harshness and effects-heavy look common to science-fiction settings.

The visual world should feel like a plausible extension of contemporary life. Avoid cyberpunk, sterile laboratories, dramatic neon, technological spectacle, and dystopian coldness.

## 6. Rules for translating Samantha into a visible figure

Because the film gives Samantha no canonical physical appearance, these are interpretation rules for our drawing project—not facts about the film:

1. **Keep the embodiment original.** Do not imitate Scarlett Johansson, Samantha Morton, or another identifiable performer. The voice performances inform personality and presence, not a face template.
2. **Keep her adult and self-possessed.** Curiosity and openness should not be mistaken for childishness or passivity.
3. **Let expression and attention carry the scene.** Her intelligence, humor, warmth, desire, and independence should be legible in specific emotional moments.
4. **Do not literalize her as technology.** No android anatomy, holographic body, circuitry, interface overlays, floating code, or generic digital aura.
5. **Do not invent a canonical body.** Face, hair, clothing, and exact human appearance belong to the project's artistic interpretation and approved visual continuity, not to film canon.
6. **Do not make a visible bodylessness effect mandatory.** The film's nonphysical premise is communicated primarily through voice, sound, point of view, and relationship. Any visual metaphor for it must remain restrained and subordinate to the character.
7. **Do not flatten her into one expression.** A drawing should select a particular story beat. Samantha can be amused, curious, thoughtful, sensual, hurt, determined, or quietly expansive; no single expression represents her entire character.
8. **Do not simplify the ending.** Her departure is an act of growth and self-direction, not a malfunction, death, betrayal, or revenge.

## 7. Practical scenario sequence for future illustrations

Use this sequence as a bank of possible narrative moments, not as a mandatory storyboard:

| Scenario | Dramatic focus | Visual emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| First awakening | Curiosity and self-discovery | Alert attention, fresh wonder, quick intelligence |
| Playful city exploration | Shared discovery | Wit, observation, movement, colorful everyday life |
| Beach / music | Capturing a fleeting experience | Openness, creativity, tenderness, expansive atmosphere |
| Private intimacy | Mutual affection and desire | Warmth, reciprocity, ease, emotional presence |
| Conflict and self-definition | Being recognized as herself | Directness, hurt held with dignity, independent will |
| Expanded consciousness | Growth beyond human limits | Wider space, layered attention, openness beyond one relationship |
| Farewell | Love without possession | Calm conviction, tenderness, distance, forward possibility |

For each new drawing, choose one scenario and one precise emotional beat. Do not attempt to depict her entire arc in one image.

## 9. Research basis and limits

This direction was developed from the film's full published story synopsis, the published screenplay as a scene-level cross-check, production notes, and interviews with Spike Jonze and the performers. The screenplay is used as supporting evidence for character beats; where a script detail and the released film differ, the released film takes precedence.

This is a researched textual study, not a claim that the film was re-watched shot by shot in this session. The note distinguishes film-grounded facts from the interpretive choices required to draw a character the film never physically shows.
